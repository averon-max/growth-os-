### 1. COST — for H2 "What should you look for in a credit monitoring service?"

> **How much does credit monitoring cost?** Credit monitoring may be
> offered free or through a paid subscription. Prices and included
> features vary, so there is no single standard monthly price. The
> Consumer Financial Protection Bureau notes that some services cost
> more than $15 per month. Before paying, check which credit bureaus
> are monitored, what triggers an alert, how often reports are checked,
> and whether cancellation fees or automatic renewal apply. A paid
> alert service should be compared with the option of reviewing your
> credit reports yourself for free.
>
> Source: [CFPB — What is a credit monitoring service?](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-credit-monitoring-service-en-1365/).
> Supplement: [FTC — What to know about identity theft](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-identity-theft).

Do not invent ranges such as $9.99–$49.99 or say "all monitoring is
paid". If the saved article already makes an unsupported specific
price claim, source-check or remove it.

### 2. ACTIVATION_AND_ACCESS — for H2 "How does credit monitoring work?"

> **How can you tell whether you already have credit monitoring?**
> Check whether you enrolled directly with a monitoring service, or
> whether coverage was offered through your bank, credit union, credit
> card, employer benefits, insurance provider, or a data-breach
> notification. Look for an enrollment confirmation and review the
> service account or benefit documents to learn which credit reports
> are included. If you are unsure, contact the provider using contact
> details from its official website; do not assume that simply having
> a credit score app means you have monitoring of all three nationwide
> credit bureaus.
>
> Source: [FTC — What to know about identity theft](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-identity-theft).
> **Review required**: the workflow tips (confirmation/account/official

### 3. FREE_AND_PAID — within existing service-choice or definition H2

> **Can you monitor your credit for free?** Yes. In the United States,
> you can review your credit reports from each of the three nationwide
> credit reporting companies weekly at no cost. This is a way to
> monitor your credit yourself; it is not the same as receiving
> automatic notifications whenever a covered change is detected.
> Some institutions and data-breach response programs also offer
> free credit monitoring with alerts, but the coverage and eligibility
> depend on the offer. Check the bureaus included, the alert types,
> and any automatic-renewal or cancellation terms before signing up.
>
> Source: [CFPB — What is a credit monitoring service?](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-credit-monitoring-service-en-1365/).
> Source: [FTC — Understanding your credit](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/understanding-your-credit).

Avoid claiming that a free report is a real-time monitoring alert.

### 4. FREEZE_AND_PROTECTION — for H2 "What are the limitations of credit monitoring?"

> **Do you still need credit monitoring when your credit is frozen?**
> A security freeze and credit monitoring serve different purposes.
> A freeze generally stops new creditors from accessing your credit
> reports, helping prevent new accounts from being opened in your
> name; monitoring watches for specified changes and alerts you
> afterward. You can freeze and unfreeze your file at each of the
> three nationwide credit bureaus for free. A freeze does not stop
> every kind of identity theft, and monitoring does not replace
> prevention or prompt action if your information is misused. You
> may choose to monitor existing credit activity even with a freeze,
> but whether a paid service is worthwhile depends on the monitoring
> coverage and your needs.
>
> Source: [CFPB — What is a credit freeze?](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-credit-freeze-or-security-freeze-on-my-credit-report-en-1341/).
> Source: [FTC — What to know about identity theft](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-identity-theft).

Do not say freeze stops all identity theft or prevents every possible
credit transaction; keep clear that this is primarily a barrier to
new-credit access.

### 5. VALUE_AND_NECESSITY / WHO_NEEDS_IT — conditional direct answer

> **Do you need credit monitoring, and is it worth paying for?**
> You do not have to buy a monitoring subscription to check your credit
> reports. In the US, you can review reports for free, and a free
> security freeze can make it harder to open new accounts using your
> identity. A monitoring service may be useful when you want
> notifications about covered changes without checking manually,
> particularly after personal data exposure or when a free service is
> provided through an existing benefit. Before deciding to pay,
> compare the number of monitored bureaus, alert coverage, total fees
> and any included identity-recovery support. Monitoring helps you
> discover certain changes; it does not prevent identity theft.
>
> Source: [FTC — What to know about identity theft](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-identity-theft).
> Source: [FTC — Understanding your credit](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/understanding-your-credit).
> Source: [CFPB — What is a credit monitoring service?](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-credit-monitoring-service-en-1365/).

This should be a short practical answer inside an existing service-choice
H2, not a generic sales pitch or an unsupported universal yes/no.
